## 1. BEGINNER PRIMER: HOW IT ACTUALLY WORKS

This section is for reps explaining the whole model from scratch to someone who has never sold anything on Amazon. Use these for any beginner lead.

### What's the actual relationship between a seller and Amazon?
Amazon is a marketplace. The student is a third-party seller. Amazon is not the student's employer and the student is not Amazon's employee. The student owns their brand entirely. Amazon takes a fee for every sale and provides the marketplace infrastructure (traffic, fulfillment via FBA, customer service, returns processing). That's it.

Amazon does not own your product, your brand, your customer relationships, or your inventory. You do.

### Does Amazon make my product?
No. The student designs and brands the product, finds a manufacturer (typically through Alibaba or our in-house sourcing agent), and the manufacturer produces the product. The student then ships their finished inventory to Amazon's warehouses. Amazon stores it and ships it to customers when orders come in.

### Do I need a website to sell on Amazon?
No. Amazon's product listing IS the storefront. Most Passion Product students don't have a separate website at launch. Some build a Shopify store later as an expansion play, but it's optional and comes after Amazon is profitable.

### Do I need to be on social media?
No, but it helps. Many of our students launch with no social media presence. That said, the Amazon-TikTok flywheel is one of the strongest current strategies, so having even a basic TikTok presence is valuable. Not required.

### Do I need a logo and brand name to start?
Yes, but they don't need to be expensive. The program covers branding and we connect students to FBA Creatives (40% off) for design work. Total brand design cost is typically around $500.

### How does the money actually flow during a launch?
The cash flow timeline for a typical launch:

1. **Pay manufacturer.** Usually a deposit (often 30%) when the order is placed, then the balance (70%) before shipping. This is the biggest single expense, typically $2K to $5K.
2. **Ship to Amazon.** Inventory arrives at Amazon's warehouse. Student is paid nothing yet.
3. **List the product live.** First sales start coming in.
4. **Amazon pays the student every 2 weeks.** First payout lands roughly 2 to 4 weeks after first sale, after Amazon's fees and any holds.
5. **Reserves loosen over time.** New sellers have a portion of revenue held back as reserve. This shrinks as the seller establishes history.
6. **Cashflow stabilizes** by month 3 to 4 in most cases.

The student is "out money" from manufacturing payment until first payout lands. That's the gap Kickstarter or financing covers for many students.

### How do customers actually find my product?
Two ways:

1. **Organic search.** Customer types a keyword into Amazon's search bar, Amazon's algorithm shows them ranked products. This is the long-term game.
2. **Paid (PPC).** The student bids on keywords and pays per click. Used at launch to drive initial sales velocity, then scaled back as organic ranking takes over.

The framework is: use PPC at launch to fund the climb to organic ranking. Once you're ranking organically, PPC becomes a smaller percentage of total sales.

### What's the difference between PPC and organic?
- **Organic sales** are free traffic from Amazon's search algorithm. Customer searched, Amazon decided to show your product, customer bought.
- **PPC sales** are paid traffic. You paid Amazon to put your product in front of that customer.

Organic is the goal. PPC is the lever to get there. Most stable Passion Product launches end up around 70% to 80% organic and 20% to 30% paid.

### What does "page one" mean and why does it matter?
When a customer searches, Amazon shows the top results on page one. **Around 70% of sales happen on page one.** Most customers never scroll to page two. Getting to page one for your main keywords is the primary goal of the launch phase.

The first 8 days post-launch are the most important window because Amazon assesses sales velocity during that time and assigns initial ranking. Strong launch = fast climb to page one. Weak launch = slow climb or stuck on page two.

### How do I price my product?
Two anchors to balance:

1. **The math floor.** Total cost (manufacturing + Amazon fees + shipping in + ad spend) must leave a real profit margin. We aim for **at least 30% net margin** after Amazon takes its cut.
2. **The market ceiling.** What competitors are charging and what the niche audience will pay. Premium positioning means pricing above the generic competition, not below.

The Passion Product method specifically uses premium pricing. We are not racing to the bottom. The whole point of niche branding is being able to charge more because the product is built for a specific person.

### Can I keep my full-time job while doing this?
Yes. **Most students do.** The framework is built for someone with a full-time job. The launch phase takes a few hours a week of focused work, and once the product is live, ongoing time commitment drops further.

### How niche is "too niche"?
Rough threshold: **300 monthly searches on the main keyword** is the floor we look for, with related keyword stacking pushing total demand higher. Below 300 monthly searches, the niche is usually too small to support a launch. Above 5,000 monthly searches, the niche is usually starting to attract competition.

The sweet spot is a niche big enough to support a $50K to $100K-a-year business and small enough that Chinese sellers don't bother. That's the entire point of niching down.

### What if my niche is too small?
A small niche is good. A $50K to $100K-a-year niche brand is life-changing for most students. Big markets attract Chinese competition and price wars. Small niches attract loyal customers. We deliberately pick small niches.

### What if the validation data says yes but my gut says no?
**Trust the data.** Gut is biased. People who let intuition override data are the people who lose money on launches. The reverse-P&L framework exists because gut decisions don't have a track record. Data does.

### What if Amazon launches an Amazon Basics version of my product?
Almost never happens to niche-branded products. Amazon Basics targets large generic categories where they can outscale everyone. They don't target $100K-a-year niches because the volume isn't worth it. This is one of the protective effects of niching down.

### What if my product breaks and a customer sues me?
Rare, but it happens. Product liability insurance is the answer. Not required at launch, but Amazon requires it once the seller hits a certain sales volume. Around $150 a month for typical coverage. Compliance team flags when it's needed.

### Will Amazon report my income to the IRS?
For US sellers: yes. Amazon issues a 1099-K once the seller passes the threshold. Most active Amazon sellers will exceed it. The student or their accountant uses the 1099-K for tax filing.

For international sellers: the W-8 BEN form on file tells Amazon the seller is a non-US person, and Amazon reports differently. Coaches walk students through the right tax setup for their country.

### Can I change my product later if it doesn't work?
Yes. Many students iterate. Version 1 launches, customer reviews come in, version 2 incorporates the feedback. This is normal and expected. The framework supports iteration.

If a product genuinely doesn't work at validation (rare), the coaches help the student pivot to a new idea before any manufacturing money is spent. That's the whole point of validation.

## 2. THE FOUR WAYS TO SELL ON AMAZON

When a lead asks about Amazon, they're almost always thinking about one of four models. Be ready to explain all four and position our method clearly against the others.

### Retail Arbitrage (RA)
Buying clearance products from retail stores (Walmart, Target, etc.) and reselling on Amazon for a markup.

**Reality:** Good way to learn the platform. Bad way to build a business. You're trading hours for dollars. Maybe $20 to $30 an hour at most. Stop sourcing, business stops. Not an asset. Not sellable.

### Wholesale
Buying brand-name products in bulk from authorized distributors and reselling on Amazon.

**Reality:** Sounds clean on paper. In practice it's a race to the bottom. Big upfront capital. Razor-thin margins. Anyone can sell the same product because you don't own the brand. Competitors undercut your price every day. No moat.

### Traditional Private Label (PL)
Finding a generic supplier product (typically on Alibaba), slapping your logo on it, and selling on Amazon at a slightly different price than competitors.

**Reality:** Worked great in 2016. Today it's overrun by Chinese sellers with their own brand registries who can manufacture at half your cost and undercut you indefinitely. Race to the bottom. **This is the model that's dead.**

### Passion Product Method (what we teach) ★
Niche branding inside saturated categories. Use Amazon's own search data to find what customers are searching for that no product exists for yet. Build a real differentiated brand that fits a specific underserved audience. Validate before launch with hard demand data. Premium positioning, not commodity pricing.

**The headline:** Generic products are saturated. Niche brands are wide open.

**Examples reps can drop:**
- "Eyebrow pencil" is saturated. "Men's eyebrow pencil designed for men's eyebrow architecture" is open.
- "Electrolytes" is saturated. "Electrolytes for the carnivore diet" did over $1M in year one.
- "Dog brush" is saturated. "Golden Retriever dog brush" does $5K a month, launched for $750.

## 3. PASSION PRODUCT METHOD vs PRIVATE LABEL

This is the most common confusion on calls. Reps need a clean answer.

### What we teach IS technically private label
The student puts their brand on a manufactured product. That's the textbook definition of private label. We use private-label manufacturing relationships, the same Alibaba supplier ecosystem, the same Amazon Brand Registry process.

### What's different is HOW we do it
- **Traditional PL:** Pick a generic product, slap a logo, compete on price. Race to the bottom against Chinese sellers.
- **Passion Product:** Pick a specific underserved audience inside a saturated category, build the product specifically for them, compete on positioning. Premium, defensible, brand-buildable.

### The simple answer for a confused lead
"Yes, this is private label. But it's private label done right. Traditional private label is dead. Niche branding is wide open. The framework difference is everything."

## 4. FBA vs FBM

Amazon offers two fulfillment models. Reps get asked which we use and why.

### FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon)
Amazon stores your inventory in their warehouses, picks and packs orders, ships them, handles returns, and handles customer service.

**Why it's the default for our students:**
- Customer service is fully off the student's plate.
- Two-day Prime shipping is automatic.
- Amazon's algorithm rewards FBA listings with better visibility.
- Once launched, the business runs on a few hours a week. The student isn't packing boxes.

### FBM (Fulfillment by Merchant)
The seller stores inventory, packs orders, ships them, and handles returns and customer service themselves.

**When FBM makes sense:**
- Oversized or unusual products that don't fit Amazon's storage.
- Products with very thin margins where FBA fees would kill profitability.
- High-touch handmade items.

For 99% of Passion Product launches, FBA is the right call. The few exceptions are obvious and the coaches handle them case-by-case.

## 6. UNGATING RESTRICTED CATEGORIES

### What "ungating" means
Amazon restricts certain categories. To list products in those categories, you need approval from Amazon. The approval process is called "getting ungated."

### Categories that are typically gated
- Health and Personal Care
- Beauty
- Grocery and Gourmet Food
- Topicals
- Some toy / kids' subcategories
- Watches and Jewelry (some)
- Automotive parts (some)

### Why this rarely matters for our students
**Ungating is mostly a Retail Arbitrage and Wholesale concern.** When you're reselling someone else's branded product in a gated category, you need approval. **Private label and Passion Product students typically don't deal with this** because they're selling their own branded product, not someone else's.

If a Passion Product student does land in a regulated category (consumables, cosmetics), the compliance team walks them through the specific approval process. Not a roadblock, just a step.

### What the rep says on a call
"Ungating mostly applies to retail arbitrage and wholesale, which isn't what we teach. For your own brand, this is rarely a real obstacle, and our compliance team handles it when it does come up."

## 7. AMAZON FEES EXPLAINED

Reps get asked "what does Amazon actually charge?" Here's the breakdown.

### Referral fee (the big one)
Amazon takes a percentage of every sale. **Typically 15%** for most categories. Varies by category from about 6% to 20%.

### FBA fulfillment fee
Charged per unit Amazon ships. Depends on size and weight. Standard size is roughly $3 to $5 per unit. Oversized items cost more.

### Monthly storage fee
For inventory sitting in Amazon's warehouses. Roughly $0.75 to $2.40 per cubic foot per month, higher in Q4 (Oct-Dec).

### Long-term storage fee
Inventory sitting in Amazon's warehouse for over 365 days incurs an aged inventory surcharge. We teach inventory management to avoid this.

### Inbound placement fee
Newer fee for sending inventory to a single Amazon warehouse vs distributing across multiple warehouses. Optional, varies.

### Removal / disposal fees
If the student needs Amazon to send unsold inventory back or destroy it. Small per-unit fee.

### Subscription fee
$39.99/month for the Professional Seller plan.

### What the rep should communicate
Total Amazon fees typically run **30 to 35% of revenue** when you add it all up. The Passion Product method specifically uses premium pricing and high margin products so this fee structure leaves real profit on the table. The race-to-the-bottom models can't survive Amazon's fees because they have no margin to begin with.

## 8. KICKSTARTER

### What Kickstarter is in this context
A pre-order crowdfunding platform. The student creates a campaign for their product, real customers pre-order before manufacturing, and that pre-order revenue funds the actual production.

### Why the program leans into Kickstarter
- **Validates demand** before any manufacturing money is spent.
- **Funds the launch** without the student putting personal capital in.
- **Builds a launch-day audience** for the Amazon launch.

### Real student wins via Kickstarter
- AJ (Cocktail Cards): raised over $100K from one viral TikTok before launching on Amazon.
- Multiple students have raised six figures this way.

### What a typical Kickstarter looks like for a student
- Pre-launch period building an email list and social audience.
- 30 to 60 day campaign on Kickstarter.
- Tiered pricing (early bird, then standard, then premium tiers).
- Manufacturer waits to produce until the campaign closes and funds clear.

### Tradeoff to understand
Kickstarter pre-buyers don't buy again on Amazon launch day. So Kickstarter funding can mean less Amazon launch-day momentum. Coaches help students decide if Kickstarter is the right path for their specific product.

## 9. TIKTOK SHOP AND THE AMAZON-TIKTOK FLYWHEEL

### What this is
TikTok Shop is TikTok's e-commerce platform. Creators promote products inside the TikTok app and shoppers buy without leaving. Amazon and TikTok Shop don't formally integrate, but they create a flywheel.

### How the flywheel works
1. Creator promotes the student's product on TikTok Shop or via affiliate.
2. About 10 to 15% of viewers who see the product on TikTok go buy it on Amazon instead of TikTok.
3. Strong Amazon sales velocity attracts more affiliates and creators.
4. More TikTok content drives more Amazon sales. Loop continues.

### Why this matters in 2026
This flywheel didn't exist a few years ago. It's a compounding traffic source on top of Amazon's organic search demand. AJ (lead coach) has done over $1M in TikTok Shop sales personally and helps students implement the flywheel.

## 10. AMAZON REVIEWS AND VINE

### How reviews work on Amazon
Customers can leave a review on any product they've purchased. Reviews are the single biggest conversion lever after the main product image. The first 50 reviews matter most. The first 100 establish credibility.

### Amazon Vine
Amazon's official program where Vine reviewers (vetted by Amazon) get free products in exchange for honest reviews. Available to Brand Registered sellers.

### How we approach early reviews
The program teaches a specific review strategy:
- Friends and family who actually want the product on launch day.
- Amazon Vine once Brand Registry is approved.
- Honest customer review request flow built into the listing experience.

We do not teach incentivized reviews, fake reviews, or any black-hat tactics. Those get accounts banned.

### Vine pricing tip
Set the product's price LOWER during the Vine review period. Reviewers judge based on the price they "paid" (even though they got it free), so a $20 perceived value gets warmer reviews than a $40 perceived value would. Once Vine reviews land, raise the price.

### Bad reviews
Worry less than leads expect. With a strong validated product and a planned review strategy from launch day, a few bad reviews don't tank a listing. The first 50 reviews defend against the future ones.

## 11. AMAZON ACCOUNT ISSUES

### Account suspension
Amazon can suspend a seller's account for various reasons (policy violations, IP complaints, performance issues, listing problems). Suspensions are stressful but usually solvable.

**What our compliance team handles:**
- Drafting and submitting Plan of Action (POA) responses.
- Working with Amazon to reinstate accounts.
- Helping move the student to a fresh account if reinstatement isn't possible.

**Rep talking point:** We have one of the most successful teams at this, and we offer it complimentary if a student is currently dealing with a suspension as part of joining the program.

### IP complaints
A brand owner files a claim against your listing alleging trademark or patent infringement. Common in the wholesale and arbitrage models, less common for Passion Product students who own their brand.

### Listing suppression
Amazon hides the listing from search for a policy or technical issue (missing required info, inappropriate keywords, etc.). Compliance team resolves these.

### Stranded inventory
Inventory sitting in Amazon warehouses that can't be sold (often due to a listing issue). Easily fixed by relisting or removing.

### Hijackers / counterfeit listings
Other sellers attaching to your product listing and selling counterfeit versions. Brand Registry stops most of this. Project Zero (advanced Brand Registry tool) gives the brand owner direct removal authority.

## 12. AMAZON TERMINOLOGY GLOSSARY

Quick reference for terms that come up on calls. Use Cmd+F.

**ASIN.** Amazon Standard Identification Number. The unique 10-character ID Amazon assigns to every product. The thing in the URL.

**SKU.** Stock Keeping Unit. The seller's internal product code. Different from ASIN.

**FNSKU.** Fulfillment Network Stock Keeping Unit. Amazon's barcode label stuck on each FBA unit so Amazon's warehouse knows which seller's inventory to ship.

**UPC / GTIN.** Universal Product Code / Global Trade Item Number. The barcode standard used outside Amazon. Required for most listings unless using GTIN exemption.

**BSR.** Best Seller Rank. Amazon's category-specific rank for each product, updated hourly. Lower number = better rank.

**A+ Content.** Enhanced product description with images, comparison charts, and brand storytelling. Available to Brand Registered sellers. Significantly improves conversion.

**Brand Registry.** Amazon's program for trademark holders. Unlocks A+ Content, Vine, Sponsored Brands ads, and IP enforcement tools. Requires a pending or approved trademark.

**Buy Box.** The featured offer on a product detail page. The "Buy Now" button buys whoever has the Buy Box. For private label products, the brand owner has the Buy Box by default.

**Sponsored Products.** Amazon's PPC ad format that targets keywords. The most common ad type. We teach this heavily.

**Sponsored Brands.** Brand-awareness ad format that runs above search results. Less ROI-focused. We don't lean into this.

**Sponsored Display.** Targeting ad format based on customer behavior and product pages. Used selectively.

**DSP (Demand Side Platform).** Amazon's programmatic advertising platform for display and video ads on and off Amazon. Mostly for advanced sellers, taught in Ascension.

**Vine.** Amazon's official early-review program. Free product in exchange for honest review.

**Coupons.** Promotional discount tool inside Seller Central. Increases conversion and visibility on search.

**Lightning Deals.** Time-limited promotion that appears on the Today's Deals page. Drives a burst of sales.

**Prime Day.** Annual Amazon shopping event in July. Massive opportunity if the student has stock and a deal ready.

**IPI (Inventory Performance Index).** Amazon's score (0 to 1000) on how efficiently the seller manages FBA inventory. Below 400 triggers storage limits.

**MCF (Multi-Channel Fulfillment).** Amazon ships your FBA inventory to customers who bought on a different channel (your Shopify store, eBay, etc.). Useful for cross-platform expansion.

**Returnless refund.** Amazon refunds the customer without requiring the product to be returned. Cheaper for low-cost items.

**Aged inventory surcharge.** Extra fee for inventory sitting in Amazon's warehouse for too long.

## 13. PRODUCT RESEARCH AND VALIDATION TERMS

These come up when a lead asks "how do you actually pick a product?"

### Search volume
The number of times people type a specific keyword into Amazon's search bar each month. The single most important demand signal.

### Stacking keywords
Adding up search volume across related keywords for the same product. A product with 300 searches on its main keyword plus 200 on three related keywords has 900 total searches. That's strong.

### CPR (Cerebro Product Rank)
Helium 10's giveaway estimate. Tells the seller roughly how many sales they'd need over 8 days to rank on page one. High CPR means high competition.

### Cerebro Score / Opportunity Score
Helium 10's metric comparing demand to competition. Rule of thumb the program teaches: **opportunity score should be at least 3x the search volume.** That signals competition is beatable.

### Eye test
Searching the keyword on Amazon and looking at page one with your own eyes. Validates what the data says or contradicts it. Always do both.

### Niching down
Making a generic product more specific to a defined audience. Same physical product, different positioning. Generic dog brush → Golden Retriever dog brush. The core skill of the Passion Product method.

### Reverse P&L
The validation framework. Use Amazon's data to project revenue, profit, and ranking before manufacturing a single unit. Decide if a product is worth launching based on real numbers, not gut feel.

## 14. CURRENT CONDITIONS (2026)

Reps need to be current on what's happening in the Amazon ecosystem right now. The lead is reading headlines too.

### Tariffs
**Currently 0%.** The Supreme Court ruled the recent tariff structure unconstitutional. **[CONFIRM with team for the most current language on this]** This removes a major risk that scared off competition for years. The window to launch is more open than it's been in a long time.

Chinese sellers dominate generic categories on Amazon. Amazon's house brand. Sometimes Amazon launches an Amazon Basics version of a popular product, killing the seller. **Almost never happens to Passion Product launches** because Amazon Basics targets high-volume generic categories, not specific niches.

### AI in product research
AI tools are changing how everyone does product research. The program uses AI in our Idea Lab to surface ideas, and AI is integrated throughout the curriculum. The framework still applies. AI just speeds up parts of the process.

## 15. INTERNATIONAL SELLING

Reps get a steady stream of leads asking if they can sell on Amazon US from outside the US.

### Yes, anyone can sell on Amazon US from anywhere
The program works with international students all the time. The setup involves:

1. **A US business entity (LLC).** Most international students form an LLC in Wyoming or Delaware via a registered agent service.
2. **A USD bank account.** Mercury, Wise, and Relay all work well for international founders. Any bank that can process USD payments is fine.
3. **W-8 BEN form.** Tax form telling Amazon the student is a non-US person for US tax purposes.
4. **An EIN (Employer Identification Number).** Required to open the LLC bank account.

The program has a dedicated legal section and a legal validation call to walk through the country-specific specifics.

### Restricted countries
A small number of countries can't sell on Amazon US at all due to Amazon's own policies (think Yemen, Somalia, similar). Almost everyone else can.

### Local marketplace strategy
International students can also sell on their local Amazon marketplace (Amazon UK, EU, CA, MX, JP, AU, etc.). Sometimes a product that's saturated in the US is wide open on a local marketplace. Coaches help students decide whether to launch US-first or local-first.

### VAT (EU)
Selling on Amazon EU requires VAT registration in the relevant countries. Adds complexity. We help students navigate it but it's not the default first move.

## 16. COMPLIANCE AND REGULATORY

### FDA
Required for supplements, food, and certain cosmetic categories. The student's manufacturer typically handles the FDA registration. Compliance team helps verify it's done right.

### Prop 65 (California)
California requires warning labels on products containing certain chemicals. Manufacturer typically handles. The compliance team verifies the student's product meets requirements.

### CPSIA (Children's Product Safety)
Toys and products for kids under 12 require CPSIA certification. Specific testing required. Compliance team walks the student through it.

### Cosmetic GMP
Good Manufacturing Practices certification for cosmetics. Manufacturer-side requirement. We connect students to compliant manufacturers.

### Product liability insurance
Not required at launch. Amazon requires it once the seller hits a certain volume threshold. Typical cost is around $150 a month for standard coverage. Compliance team flags when it's needed.

### Trademark, copyright, patent
- **Trademark:** Protects your brand name and logo. Required for Brand Registry. ~$350 to file with USPTO. We recommend filing yourself first.
- **Copyright:** Protects creative work (packaging design, marketing copy, etc.). Less common for Amazon sellers.
- **Patent:** Protects an invention. Expensive, time-consuming, hard to enforce. Rarely worth it for Amazon sellers. We almost never recommend.

## 17. BUSINESS SETUP

### LLC vs Sole Proprietorship
- **Sole prop:** Free, simple, but no liability protection. The student's personal assets are exposed.
- **LLC:** Around $150 to set up. Liability protection. Cleaner taxes. Recommended for most serious sellers.

We walk students through what makes the most sense based on their specific situation. Some students do it themselves, others go through one of our partner services. We don't push a one-size-fits-all answer.

### S-Corp election
Tax status an LLC can elect once profit is high enough (typically $50K+ in profit). Reduces self-employment tax. Discuss with the student's accountant when they get there. Not relevant on the sales call.

### EIN
Federal tax ID. Free from the IRS. Required to open a business bank account.

### State of incorporation
For US-based students, usually their home state.
For international students, **Wyoming or Delaware** are the recommended LLC states. Both are business-friendly with low ongoing costs.

### Sales tax / Marketplace Facilitator laws
Amazon collects and remits sales tax for sellers in all US states under Marketplace Facilitator laws. The student doesn't typically need to handle sales tax themselves on Amazon sales. Some states still require registration if the student exceeds a threshold. We help students navigate this.

## 18. PAYOUTS AND CASH FLOW

### Amazon's payout schedule
Amazon pays sellers **every 2 weeks** by default. Some sellers can request more frequent payouts after establishing history.

### Reserves and holds
Amazon holds back a portion of sales for a few weeks to cover potential returns. Standard for new sellers. Loosens up over time.

### What a payout actually looks like
After Amazon's fees, returns, and reserves, sellers typically see 50 to 65% of gross revenue land in their bank account. The rest is fees and reserves.

### 1099-K
Amazon sends a 1099-K to US sellers who exceed thresholds. The student or their accountant uses this for tax filing.

### Mid-launch payout holds
Sometimes Amazon imposes a payout hold during a launch (often after a sudden sales spike). The compliance team helps work through it. Usually resolves in days to weeks. Cash flow can get tight; the team helps the student navigate it.

## 19. HOW MUCH WORK IS RUNNING AN AMAZON BUSINESS

Common rep question: "Once I'm launched, what does the actual work look like?"

### Realistic minimum during the program (the build phase)
**Under 5 hours a week is enough.** Just a few dedicated, focused hours spent on the right tasks. Nothing magical about more hours: students putting in 10+ hours move faster, but students putting in 3 to 5 focused hours still get there. **[FAIR GAME]**

### During the launch (first 8 weeks live)
- A few hours a week, scaling with how aggressive the student wants to be.
- Daily PPC monitoring and optimization (10 to 20 minutes a day is enough).
- Reviewing reviews and customer messages.
- Inventory check-ins.
- Pre-planned content posting (TikTok, etc.) if doing the flywheel.

### After launch is stable
- 2 to 5 hours a week typical.
- Inventory reorder when running low.
- PPC bid adjustments weekly.
- Quarterly listing optimization passes.

### Why the time commitment is low
Amazon FBA handles all the actual fulfillment work. Picking. Packing. Shipping. Customer service. Returns. The student is a brand owner, not a warehouse manager. That's the asset.

The more hours a student puts in, the faster they move. That's the only relationship between time and progress. There's no minimum hour requirement, only a willingness to follow the framework.

## 20. CROSS-PLATFORM AND EXPANSION

Reps get asked "can I take my Amazon product to other channels?"

### Amazon → TikTok Shop
Yes, and we lean into this. Same product, different audience, viral potential. Amazon-TikTok flywheel covered above.

### Amazon → Shopify
Yes. Once a product is validated and selling on Amazon, the student can launch a Shopify store with the same product to capture margin (no Amazon fees) and own customer data. Common second move after initial Amazon success.

### Amazon → Walmart, Target, Etsy
All possible. Walmart Marketplace is the closest analog to Amazon. Etsy works for handmade or design-heavy products. Target rarely worth it for new sellers.

### Amazon US → Amazon EU, UK, CA
Yes. Local marketplaces sometimes have less competition. Specific tax and compliance setup per region (VAT in EU). Coaches help students decide if and when.

## 21. THINGS TO FLAG TO THE TEAM

Questions reps should escalate rather than answer themselves:

1. Specific FDA, CPSIA, or category-regulatory questions beyond the framework above. **Escalate to compliance team.**
2. Specific tax advice (state-by-state sales tax thresholds, S-corp election timing, country-specific tax setup). **Tell the lead to verify with their accountant.**
3. Specific patent / IP infringement situations. **Escalate to compliance team.**
4. Account suspensions in progress for the lead's existing Amazon account. **Compliance team takes over.**
5. Whether a specific competitor brand is suable. **Not our lane.**
6. International tax structure for high-net-worth international sellers. **Escalate to legal team.**
7. Anything specifically asking "is this product allowed on Amazon" for a non-standard product. **Hard program exclusions: CBD, kratom, vape, weapons, regulated chemicals.** We do not work with these. Be direct with the lead. For anything else borderline (adult products, firearms accessories, tobacco, etc.), the rule is **whatever Amazon's terms allow, we work with**. Escalate any genuine gray area to the compliance team.
