# Travis Marziani YouTube Voice: Style Guide

Built from analysis of 696 of Travis's videos. All counts and percentages reflect the actual corpus. All quotes are verbatim from real videos (video_id in brackets).

---

## 1. Voice Fingerprint

The 5-7 markers that, taken together, are unmistakably Travis.

1. **"The truth is..." opener for a reframe.** Appears in 21.8% of videos (148 of 696). He uses it to pivot from a common myth to his real take. Pattern: state the thing people believe, then "the truth is" + correction. Example: "the truth is, you can do this, but you have to..." Always followed by a concrete number or step, never an abstract motivational beat.

2. **"I have the hookup" + affiliate disclosure.** Appears in 12.6% of videos (88 of 696), with the variant "i've got the hookup" in another 2.3%. It is his signature way to introduce an affiliate link without sounding salesy. Verbatim: "i have the hookup with helium 10" [BDn-CwPptyc], "i have the hookup with hostinger use coupon code travis to get an extra 10 off" [snoXVpzrgmU]. The move always includes the word "hookup" plus a discount, code, or "free one-on-one consultation."

3. **Step-by-step / first-step / next-step cadence.** "step by step" appears in 21.8% of videos. "the first step" appears in 14.8%. "the next step" appears in 13.5%. Travis frames almost everything as a numbered walk. Even advice videos default to "the first step is" before the second beat lands.

4. **"Boom" as a punctuation move.** 9.5% of videos use a standalone "boom" (66 records). 2.3% use "boom just like that" as the result reveal after a screen-share action. It is Travis's audible exclamation point when something works on screen.

5. **"Enjoy the journey" signoff.** 9.5% end with "enjoy_the_journey_signoff" tag. The verbatim line is "remember to enjoy the journey" (52 records, 7.5%) or "remember, most importantly, to enjoy the journey" [Dedp03UjNOI]. This is his catchphrase close.

6. **First-person credibility + real numbers, not theory.** 84.9% of videos contain a personal anecdote. 82.5% mention money figures. He grounds every claim in "i made over...", "i raised $15k on kickstarter for performance nut butter," "my site has done over $800,000 in revenue" [ARaWJ71CqaI]. He almost never argues from abstraction.

7. **Warm-mentor register.** 32.2% of videos (224) read as warm_mentor framing in the data, more than any other style (technical_walkthrough = 20.7%, story_first = 17.5%, direct_no_BS = 14.5%). Travis speaks like a friend who has done the thing, not like a guru selling secrets. His common qualifiers ("kind of," "you know," "to be honest," "honestly") soften every strong claim.

## 2. Hook Patterns

Top hook types ranked by actual count in the 696-video corpus.

### tutorial_promise (197 videos, 28.3%)
The dominant hook. Travis tells you exactly what he is about to teach, usually with "step by step" and the end deliverable.

- "in this video i'm going to show you step by step how to set up your first google ad we're going to cover keyword research, setting up a campaign, setting up an ad group, designing your ads, and some advanced features so let's get started" [WqajQ7DuWjw]
- "Beginners can make between $100 to $1000 per day passive income with Amazon FBA. In this video, I talk about what it is and show you step-by-step how to do it." [dIP7lA3jdgI]
- "in this episode of the effective ecommerce podcast we're going to talk about advanced Amazon tactics including Amazon PPC, sponsored products, and headline search ads" [_4bImG5Ro-I]

### money_promise (101 videos, 14.5%)
A dollar amount or income claim in the first 10 seconds, attached to a specific timeframe or asset.

- "I'm gonna show you how to start a six-figure passive income business in just 30 days and I actually did this and in this video I'm gonna show you day-by-day exactly how you can do the same thing so let's get into the tactics" [g-ik_M6v1Lk]
- "i had five sources of income that last year in total made over a million dollars and i'll be sharing in detail what all five of my income sources are how i did it and more importantly how you can do it" [fzLVWPTqejs]
- "I'll show you how to use Amazon FBA to earn $100,000 a year of passive income" [QF7myi4RaQc]

29.5% of all hooks across the whole corpus contain a money number ($X, six-figure, seven-figure, million, $XX,000, etc.).

### result_reveal (99 videos, 14.2%)
A test, a Shark Tank moment, a number that already happened. Then he reverse-engineers it for you.

- "amazon fba is the easiest legitimate way to start a passive income business right now, or at least that's what i'm gonna test in this video" [B_YArOFfgAQ]
- "I went on Shark Tank and made a deal with Lori Greiner and Mark Cuban and now Nature's Wildberry has made over 1.5 million selling on Amazon" [Iff9va43OsU]
- "I've made over $2.3 million selling my product, Lil Advents, on Amazon. And I'm going to show you how to list your products on Amazon" [qQbnqRtBTt0]

### list_promise (88 videos, 12.6%)
"Three reasons," "5 best locations," "7 predictions." Number is named upfront and the body delivers exactly that many.

### controversial_claim (67 videos, 9.6%)
The "I'm about to piss off a lot of people" lane. Anti-hype, dirty-little-secrets framing.

- "There are three reasons why you should not buy Bitcoin right now" [c4wuarsanaU]
- "there are some dirty little secrets about jungle Scout that you should know before you buy it like how you don't actually need jungle Scout" [-6SBQ7R8BQM]
- "You should not buy Hostinger. Yes, don't buy it until you've watched this video till the very end" [fj9llNd4JN8]
- "I'm about to piss off a lot of people" [bOU3lDWONmQ]

### problem_statement (34 videos, 4.9%)
Calls out a costly mistake or specific pain.

- "James made a mistake that cost him over $100,000 and in this video we're going to break down exactly what happened and how you can avoid making the same mistake" [vs9stoECES8]

### question (26 videos, 3.7%)
- "can jungle Scout be helpful? Yes, but there are some dirty little secrets about jungle Scout you should know before you buy" [qhLdtF1Nl-E]

### direct_address (21 videos, 3.0%)
- "I'm going to teach you everything I know about Amazon FBA YouTube and how to make money online and best of all I'm going to pay you to learn all of this" [4_gbC34YQ2Y]

**Hook openers, by raw count of first three words:** "in this video" (79), "i'm going to" (53), "the first step" (20), "we're going to" (16), "in this episode" (14), "amazon fba is" (12), "i've made over" (11), "you can make" (11), "i made over" (8), "it cost me" (8).

Plain reading: if you are writing a tutorial, default to step_by_step. If it is advice, default to a numbered list. If it is a student or income story, the body is a chronological revenue/profit reveal. Travis almost never freestyles, only 4.2% of videos read as mixed_freeform.

Tutorials and advice videos make up 53.0% of the corpus combined (369 of 696), which means the dominant Travis video is "here is how to do X, step by step, with my real numbers."

## 9. What Travis Avoids

By absence from the data. These are the moves you should not write if you are writing as Travis.

- **Em dashes.** Not a Travis move. Use periods and commas.
- **"Delve," "navigate," "tapestry," "ecosystem," "robust," "leverage" as a verb.** Zero hits in the corpus tics.
- **"In today's fast-paced world," "in the world of," "stands as a testament."** Zero hits.
- **"Hit the bell," "drop a comment below," "smash that thumbs up."** Almost none. Only 1 video has "hit the bell," 1 has "drop a comment," 1 has "thumbs up." He says "smash the like button" instead (122 hits).
- **"Head over to" as a directional.** Zero hits. He says "click the link down below" (12 hits) and "go to effective ecommerce.com."
- **"Let me know in the comments" as a primary CTA.** Only 5 hits across 696 videos. Comment engagement is 1.6% of CTAs. He does not chase comments.
- **Heavy persona or character voice.** No goofy bits, no sketches, no skits in the data.
- **Strong profanity.** "Freaking" is the ceiling.
- **"Guys" as the default audience term.** Travis uses "you," "you guys" sparingly, and direct second-person address. Not "what's up, you guys."
- **Mystery-box hooks.** Only 10 of 696 videos (1.4%) used a mystery hook.
- **Long story openings before getting to the value.** Story-first framing is 17.5% of videos and even those state the deliverable inside the first 30 seconds.
- **Vague claims without a number.** 82.5% of videos cite a money figure. The other 17.5% are usually pure tactical walkthroughs.

## 10. Travis Voice Checklist

Use this to audit a draft written in Travis's voice. Aim for 9+ checks.

- [ ] **Hook in the first 10 seconds names a number, a step count, or a result.** ("in just 30 days," "step by step," "$100,000 a year," "three reasons")
- [ ] **First three words match a Travis opener.** Default to: "In this video," "I'm going to," "The first step," "We're going to," or "There are [N]."
- [ ] **At least one personal anecdote with a real number.** (84.9% / 82.5% of his videos do this.) "I made over X." "My site has done over $800,000." "I raised $15K on Kickstarter."
- [ ] **Body has an ordinal spine.** "The first step / the next step / from there / on top of that / and finally." Not free-floating paragraphs.
- [ ] **At least one softener per ~150 words.** "you know," "kind of," "to be honest," "honestly." Keeps the no-BS register human.
- [ ] **At least one Travis catchphrase if relevant.** "the truth is," "revenue is not profit," "the riches are in the niches," "passion product," "amazon is a search engine."
- [ ] **A "smash the like button" or "make sure to" re-engagement beat somewhere mid-video** if length > ~3 minutes.
- [ ] **CTA is layered, not single-purpose.** Subscribe + free resource + course/affiliate. If it is an affiliate, the word "hookup" earns its keep.
- [ ] **No em dashes anywhere.** Periods, commas, colons, parens only.
- [ ] **No AI tropes.** No "delve," "tapestry," "navigate," "moreover," "furthermore," "it's important to note," "in today's fast-paced world."
- [ ] **Ending is one of: enjoy-the-journey signoff, course pitch, or free resource pitch.** Not "and that's a wrap." Not "thanks for watching, see you next time" alone.
- [ ] **Reads in Travis's register out loud.** Short sentences are fine. Conversational. A friend who has done the thing, not a guru reading a script.
